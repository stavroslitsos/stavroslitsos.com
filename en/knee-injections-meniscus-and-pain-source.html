Knee Injections and Meniscal Pain – Are We Addressing the Right Problem?

When a knee injection does not help, it does not necessarily mean the needle missed. The medication may have reached the joint, while the treatment was not well matched to the pain mechanism – or any effect was brief. Diagnosis comes before procedure.

Accurate placement is not the same as the right treatment

Ultrasound guidance improves the average accuracy of intra-articular knee injections compared with landmarks alone. That tells us about technique, not that every patient will experience lasting pain relief. Knee symptoms may involve the synovium, subchondral bone, meniscus, tendons or other tissues.

For knee osteoarthritis, NICE prioritises core care and suggests considering intra-articular corticosteroid when other medicines are unsuitable or ineffective, or to support therapeutic exercise. People should be told relief is usually short term, around 2–10 weeks. Repeated frequent injections are not an obvious solution: in a two-year randomised trial, injections every three months caused more cartilage loss than saline without a significant pain advantage.

What about an injection around the meniscus?

The video draws attention to tissue near the meniscal edge and proposes a small ultrasound-guided injection there. A case series of 32 people with knee osteoarthritis and meniscal extrusion, and an anatomical feasibility study, show that the approach can be performed and some patients reported short-term improvement. Without robust comparison groups, they cannot show it works better than a joint injection, exercise or natural recovery.

Meniscal changes on MRI need not be the main driver of symptoms. In a five-year randomised trial of degenerative tears, exercise-based physical therapy was non-inferior to arthroscopic partial meniscectomy for patient-reported function. A local injection does not repair a structural tear and does not replace assessment of locking, injury or major functional loss.

Making a shared decision

Before injection, clarify the goal: short-term relief to enable movement, or treatment of another defined condition? Pain location alone cannot reliably identify the responsible tissue. Discuss potential benefit, risks, uncertainty and alternatives, then agree how function and response will be reviewed.

How I approach it

I begin with the history and examination. If injection is appropriate, precision is a means, not proof of the diagnosis; activity and follow-up remain part of the plan.
