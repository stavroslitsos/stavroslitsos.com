Shoulder Calcium Deposits – What Do They Mean for Treatment?

A calcium deposit in a shoulder tendon may look striking on a scan, but its size or position alone cannot explain why a shoulder hurts. The condition can evolve, so treatment decisions should follow the clinical picture rather than an isolated bright spot on ultrasound.

What is calcific tendinopathy?

Calcium-containing material accumulates in or around a rotator cuff tendon. Formation, resting and resorptive phases are useful descriptions in the literature, but a single scan cannot establish an exact biological stage for an individual. Ultrasound can help assess the deposit's appearance and whether it lies within the tendon, near the bursa or in surrounding tissue.

Pain may develop gradually or abruptly. A deposit that is breaking down can irritate nearby tissue and cause severe symptoms. Deposits may also be present without proportional pain. Movement, loading, sleep disturbance and alternative shoulder diagnoses therefore matter as well.

A plausible theory is not yet a treatment rule

The video suggests that deposits originate near the tendon–bone attachment and later migrate. This is an interesting mechanistic hypothesis, not an established explanation for every deposit. Imaging reviews describe several migration patterns without proving the origin of a particular patient's calcification.

Distinguishing a compact deposit from dispersed material can inform discussion, but it does not provide a validated algorithm that says one needle technique helps in one stage and harms in another. Such precise promises would exceed the evidence.

What about treatment?

Appropriately adjusted activity, symptom relief and progressive rehabilitation often come first. Shockwave therapy may help some patients, although systematic reviews report heterogeneous studies and uncertainty. One sham-controlled trial found no overall added benefit of radial shockwaves alongside supervised exercise for subacromial pain; a prespecified calcification subgroup showed a possible benefit.

Ultrasound-guided lavage is not a guaranteed solution either. A Norwegian–Swedish sham-controlled BMJ trial involving 220 adults found no advantage of lavage with corticosteroid over sham treatment during follow-up. Other studies and syntheses have produced differing results. Expected benefit, possible downsides, natural history and the patient's goals should be discussed before an invasive procedure.

When to reassess

Persistent substantial pain, marked weakness, fever or a new injury warrants reassessment of the diagnosis and plan. The aim is not to remove calcium at any cost, but to explain the finding accurately and track pain and function over time. In long-term follow-up many deposits resolved, without resolution necessarily explaining recovery.

How I approach it

I bring together the history, a clinical examination and focused ultrasound when appropriate. I explain what the deposit shows – and what imaging cannot establish – before agreeing a plan centred on function and the patient's goals.
